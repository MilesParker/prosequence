Fare-Free Transit and Operator Safety

Assaults on bus operators have become one of the most persistent hazards in
urban transit work. According to a 2023 report by the Transit Workers Safety
Coalition, fare disputes are the single most common trigger of physical
altercations between passengers and drivers, accounting for a large share of
reported operator assaults across surveyed North American agencies.

When fares are eliminated, the driver is no longer placed in the role of an
enforcement officer at the door. The Coalition's report documents that agencies
which adopted fare-free service saw a marked drop in operator-directed assaults
in the following year, as the most frequent flashpoint for conflict simply
disappeared.

Removing fares therefore improves the working conditions of transit operators
by removing a recurring source of on-the-job violence. A workforce that is safer
and less exposed to assault is also a more stable one, which supports reliable
service over time.

More broadly, a transit system should not require its front-line workers to
absorb physical risk in order to collect small sums of money at the door. The
burden of fare enforcement falls hardest on the workers least able to refuse it.
